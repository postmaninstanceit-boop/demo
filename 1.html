With a 17-mile ring buried as deep as 574 feet under the French-Swiss border, the world's largest particle accelerator is an underground "city" built to push physics to its limit.

The Cold Machine Under Europe

The LHC uses a ring of superconducting magnets in a tunnel with an average depth of 328 feet underground near Geneva. To make that possible, the magnets must be kept at 1.9 kelvin — about minus 456 degrees Fahrenheit. That is colder than outer space, and it is why the LHC is as much an engineering story as a physics story.

Why Smash Protons?

The best-known result came in July 2012, when the Higgs boson was discovered — confirming the final predicted piece of the Standard Model.

A 56-Mile Successor

The feasibility report describes a preferred 56.4-mile ring at an average depth of about 656 feet. The plan has two stages. First, FCC-ee would study the Higgs boson with extreme precisionPrecision (n.) — the quality of being exact, accurate, and careful in detail. Later, FCC-hh would collide protons at around 100 TeV — far beyond today's LHC energy.

The Money and the Footprint

CERN's report estimates the construction cost of the FCC electron-positron stage at roughly $19 billion. What Happens Next

The FCC is not approved yet. A decision on construction is expected around 2028.
